# PatioFinder Privacy Policy

*Last updated: July 11, 2026*

PatioFinder ("the app") is made by Justin Nikolaus. This policy explains what
information the app uses and why, in plain language.

## The short version

PatioFinder does not have accounts, does not show ads, does not use
analytics or trackers, and does not sell or share your data with anyone for
marketing purposes. The only information it uses is your device's location,
and only to answer the question "what's the nearest patio?"

## What we collect

**Location.** While PatioFinder is open and in use, it reads your device's
GPS location and compass heading. This is used entirely on your device to:

- Point the on-screen arrow toward the nearest outdoor patio
- Calculate the live distance and walking ETA shown in the app
- Sort and filter the list of nearby patios

Your coordinates are sent to two outside services, solely to perform the
searches described above:

- **Google Places API**, to find nearby restaurants with outdoor seating,
  their ratings, and whether they're currently open
- **Apple WeatherKit**, to show the current temperature and conditions near
  you

Location is also used with **Apple MapKit** as a fallback search when Google
Places has no results, and to open Apple Maps for walking directions when
you tap an address.

We do not collect your name, email address, contacts, photos, or any other
personal information. PatioFinder has no login, no account system, and no
way to identify you as an individual user.

## What we don't do

- No accounts or sign-in
- No advertising or ad networks
- No analytics, crash reporting, or usage tracking
- No third-party SDKs bundled in the app
- No sale or sharing of your data for marketing or advertising purposes
- No storage of your location history — each search uses your current
  location in the moment and is not retained by PatioFinder afterward

## Third-party services

Because PatioFinder queries Google and Apple's services to do its job, your
location data passing through those queries is subject to their own privacy
policies, which we don't control:

- Google Places API — see [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy)
- Apple MapKit / WeatherKit — see [Apple Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/)

## Location permission

iOS will ask you to allow location access "while using the app." You can
change this anytime in **Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services >
PatioFinder**. If you decline or revoke access, PatioFinder can't locate
nearby patios and will fall back to its bundled sample list.

## Children's privacy

PatioFinder is rated 4+ and does not knowingly collect personal information
from anyone, including children.
